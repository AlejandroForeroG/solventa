# Approved claim disbursements

Code: `application/claim-disbursements.ts`, `adapters/outbound/sql-claim-disbursements.ts` and the existing synthetic payment gateway. This increment processes one complete, analyst-authorized payment for an approved manually reviewed mortgage-life claim. Flight-delay and weather coverages retain their existing [automatic parametric engine](parametric-payouts.md); the manual endpoint rejects them so the two engines cannot pay the same coverage independently.

## Authorization and amount

The operator uses `POST /api/v1/claims/{claimId}/disbursement` with only `{ "amount": "1000.0000" }`. The server verifies a fresh `claims:decide` grant through Identity, the case assignment, a persisted approval, an issued policy and the separation between analyst and policyholder. No caller supplies a recipient, payment identifier, policy, decision or provider reference. A foreign or self-owned case returns an opaque 404. The operator reads the durable state through `GET` on the same path with a fresh `claims:read` grant and the same assignment boundary.

Amounts are positive canonical COP decimals with four fraction digits. The signed accepted-offer coverage and issuance evidence are verified before use. The approved amount cannot exceed the coverage remaining after other pending or paid indemnities. The policy and claim are locked in the authorization transaction, so two analysts' concurrent claims cannot reserve the same remaining coverage. The displayed claim estimate is never used as payment authorization.

The same locked context includes the existing append-only policy lifecycle record. Its schema, policy/document binding, stored change identity/time and HMAC seal are verified. A new payment returns conflict if the occurrence is at or after the contractual end recorded by cancellation, modification or renewal; an earlier covered occurrence remains eligible. An already accepted intent retains its stable replay and recovery after a later lifecycle change. The cutoff is not reapplied as a cancellation of that durable financial obligation. Decision capture rule version 2 includes the actual lifecycle record or null; historical version 1 captures remain unchanged. Invalid lifecycle evidence fails closed before any provider call. No migration or extra runtime grant is needed.

The immutable approval supplies the idempotency identity, `approved-claim:<decisionId>`. An identical amount reuses the same payment, including after a browser retry or restart; a different amount conflicts. No browser-provided idempotency identifier can create a second payment for the approval. Partial installments, amended approvals and a new authorization after a terminal decline are outside this increment.

## Durable intent and recovery

The existing `indemnities` and `payments` tables hold the intent. Their existing unique payment key is scoped to policy and operation. This path does not insert into the parametric `financial_payment_intents` slot, so separate approved claims do not consume or overwrite that engine's per-policy identity. No migration or additional runtime privilege is required.

Authorization commits the indemnity, pending payment, audit and historical decision capture/outbox in one Policy transaction. The payment content is HMAC-bound to its identity, policy, approval key, currency and approved amount. A 202 response means this durable intent was accepted; it never means the recipient was paid. The HTTP path attaches immediate processing to `ctx.waitUntil`. If that work is interrupted or its response is lost, the existing minute schedule selects pending manual intents independently of the legacy issuance and parametric recovery batches. Batches are bounded to 25 and refresh their selection age to avoid repeatedly starving later intents. No new Queue, Worker or external provider is provisioned.

Every processing attempt looks up the same payment at the synthetic provider before settlement. Unknown results allow settlement with that stable identity; lookup failure, a hard 700 ms provider deadline or an uncertain settlement leaves the local payment pending. The underlying provider operation may complete after a timeout, so the next attempt looks it up rather than assuming no effect occurred. The durable mock ledger enforces one effect per payment and rejects changed content. This is application recovery with a simulated provider, not real bank settlement.

A confirmed result is committed with payment and indemnity state, the claim's `paid` transition, audit, capture/outbox and participant notices in one transaction. A local commit failure keeps the pending intent, and recovery reconciles the provider's already confirmed receipt without a second financial effect. A declined result is terminal, does not mark the claim paid and leaves no confirmed-payment notice. Concurrent processing cannot overwrite an already terminal state. Expiry of an operator's session does not cancel an intent accepted under valid authorization; subsequent reads still require a fresh grant. Late authorization transaction responses cannot release protected state after their lease expires.

Participant notices are durable case records for the customer and assigned analyst. The case-file contract includes `disbursement_confirmed` and `disbursement_declined` alongside review actions. They do not establish external email, push or native notification delivery. The additional outbox records preserve historical captures; scheduled payment recovery reads the owner intent directly and does not depend on an unimplemented generic outbox publisher.

## Operator and customer channels

The assigned approved case exposes an amount form in the web console. Input accepts a decimal comma or point without grouping, is converted exactly to four fraction digits and requires explicit amount review before authorization. The server remains authoritative for eligibility and remaining coverage. A failed initial read cannot enable a payment. An uncertain command freezes the original amount for retry; a confirmed or declined receipt cannot start another authorization. Pending receipts are reread every five seconds while visible, and only a confirmed receipt refreshes the case as paid. Backgrounding hides the protected view; returning reads fresh state.

Customer web and native screens read `GET /api/v1/claims/customer/disbursements` and `GET /api/v1/claims/native/disbursements`, respectively. Web uses the existing sealed session and `claims:respond` ownership operation; native uses the registered mobile bearer session and `claims:report`. Neither grants operator access. SQL filters both client and subject before decoding evidence, and the application repeats the owner check and validates signed issuance/payment integrity. Reads never create or reconcile a payment. They return at most six receipts ordered by claim UUID, an optional opaque UUID `cursor` and `next`, or an empty page. Foreign or missing cursor boundaries yield no records. Unknown, duplicated or invalid query parameters fail with 400 after authorization. Responses use `Cache-Control: no-store`; unavailable authorization or persistence fails closed.

Each receipt includes the actual claim number, exact COP amount, pending/confirmed/declined status, explicitly simulated provider label and payment update timestamp. Only confirmed payments carry a provider reference. The timestamp is the stored payment row update time, not a guaranteed settlement date. A selection by scheduled recovery may also update a pending row's timestamp. No bank account, deductible, transfer share action or external settlement evidence is fabricated. See the [versioned API](../../../packages/contracts/openapi/v1/claims.yaml) and [examples](../../../packages/contracts/examples/claim-payments.json).

The native Claims tile opens My indemnities. Its focused screen holds receipts only in memory, clears them when backgrounded, unfocused, unmounted or denied, and rereads through the expected owner session on return. It adds no device storage or offline financial copy. Web and native pagination and refresh retain no receipt after a failed replacement read. Both distinguish empty data, expired authentication, access denial and an unavailable service. General claim timeline, requirement responses and partial installments remain separate capabilities.

## Verification and limits

Run `npm run test:claim-disbursements`, `npm run test:claim-disbursements:sql` with local SQL running, mobile lint/types, and `npm run check`. CI includes the real SQL suite. The SQL test creates its own synthetic database, applies the current unchanged Policy catalog and minimum runtime grants, and checks access, stable replay, concurrent coverage reservations, provider-confirmed/local-rollback recovery, atomic terminal records, post-commit authorization expiry and owner-filtered pagination without provider effects. In-memory tests cover provider failures/deadlines, decline, changed financial/issuance evidence, bearer-only owner reads and background/late-response clearing. SPA tests exercise amount review, same-amount retry and failed receipt reads; Pact verifies both channels against the actual HTTP handlers. These layers are distinct from an authenticated deployed analyst/customer journey. Native acceptance additionally requires a device/emulator run with the matching backend, account isolation, background/return and a fresh confirmed receipt; a web export does not establish native acceptance.

The financial availability objective is at least 99.99% monthly. Passing a finite fault/retry test does not establish that monthly objective, regional failover or external settlement. Retain environment, revision, load, observation window and confirmed-versus-pending counters when measuring the live flow. These checks do not establish a monthly availability measurement or completion of the entire claims feature.
