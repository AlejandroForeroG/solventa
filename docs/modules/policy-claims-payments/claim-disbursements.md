# Approved claim disbursements

Code: `application/claim-disbursements.ts`, `adapters/outbound/sql-claim-disbursements.ts` and the existing synthetic payment gateway. This increment processes one complete, analyst-authorized payment for an approved manually reviewed mortgage-life claim. Flight-delay and weather coverages retain their existing [automatic parametric engine](parametric-payouts.md); the manual endpoint rejects them so the two engines cannot pay the same coverage independently.

## Authorization and amount

The operator uses `POST /api/v1/claims/{claimId}/disbursement` with only `{ "amount": "1000.0000" }`. The server verifies a fresh `claims:decide` grant through Identity, the case assignment, a persisted approval, an issued policy and the separation between analyst and policyholder. No caller supplies a recipient, payment identifier, policy, decision or provider reference. A foreign or self-owned case returns an opaque 404. The operator reads the durable state through `GET` on the same path with a fresh `claims:read` grant and the same assignment boundary. These are operator APIs; this increment does not add a customer or native tracking screen.

Amounts are positive canonical COP decimals with four fraction digits. The signed accepted-offer coverage and issuance evidence are verified before use. The approved amount cannot exceed the coverage remaining after other pending or paid indemnities. The policy and claim are locked in the authorization transaction, so two analysts' concurrent claims cannot reserve the same remaining coverage. The displayed claim estimate is never used as payment authorization.

The immutable approval supplies the idempotency identity, `approved-claim:<decisionId>`. An identical amount reuses the same payment, including after a browser retry or restart; a different amount conflicts. No browser-provided idempotency identifier can create a second payment for the approval. Partial installments, amended approvals and a new authorization after a terminal decline are outside this increment.

## Durable intent and recovery

The existing `indemnities` and `payments` tables hold the intent. Their existing unique payment key is scoped to policy and operation. This path does not insert into the parametric `financial_payment_intents` slot, so separate approved claims do not consume or overwrite that engine's per-policy identity. No migration or additional runtime privilege is required.

Authorization commits the indemnity, pending payment, audit and historical decision capture/outbox in one Policy transaction. The payment content is HMAC-bound to its identity, policy, approval key, currency and approved amount. A 202 response means this durable intent was accepted; it never means the recipient was paid. The HTTP path attaches immediate processing to `ctx.waitUntil`. If that work is interrupted or its response is lost, the existing minute schedule selects pending manual intents independently of the legacy issuance and parametric recovery batches. Batches are bounded to 25 and refresh their selection age to avoid repeatedly starving later intents. No new Queue, Worker or external provider is provisioned.

Every processing attempt looks up the same payment at the synthetic provider before settlement. Unknown results allow settlement with that stable identity; lookup failure, a hard 700 ms provider deadline or an uncertain settlement leaves the local payment pending. The underlying provider operation may complete after a timeout, so the next attempt looks it up rather than assuming no effect occurred. The durable mock ledger enforces one effect per payment and rejects changed content. This is application recovery with a simulated provider, not real bank settlement.

A confirmed result is committed with payment and indemnity state, the claim's `paid` transition, audit, capture/outbox and participant notices in one transaction. A local commit failure keeps the pending intent, and recovery reconciles the provider's already confirmed receipt without a second financial effect. A declined result is terminal, does not mark the claim paid and leaves no confirmed-payment notice. Concurrent processing cannot overwrite an already terminal state. Expiry of an operator's session does not cancel an intent accepted under valid authorization; subsequent reads still require a fresh grant. Late authorization transaction responses cannot release protected state after their lease expires.

Participant notices are durable case records for the customer and assigned analyst. They do not establish external email, push or native notification delivery. The additional outbox records preserve historical captures; scheduled payment recovery reads the owner intent directly and does not depend on an unimplemented generic outbox publisher.

## Verification and limits

Run `npm run test:claim-disbursements`, `npm run test:claim-disbursements:sql` with local SQL running, and `npm run check`. CI includes the real SQL suite. The SQL test creates its own synthetic database, applies the current unchanged Policy catalog and minimum runtime grants, and checks access, stable replay, concurrent coverage reservations, provider-confirmed/local-rollback recovery, atomic terminal records and post-commit authorization expiry. In-memory tests cover provider failures/deadlines, decline and changed financial/issuance evidence. These layers are distinct from an authenticated deployed analyst/customer journey.

The financial availability objective is at least 99.99% monthly. Passing a finite fault/retry test does not establish that monthly objective, regional failover or external settlement. Retain environment, revision, load, observation window and confirmed-versus-pending counters when measuring the live flow. This backend increment does not claim a new monthly availability measurement, deployed business acceptance, a native receipt screen or completion of the entire claims feature.
